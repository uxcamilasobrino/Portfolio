Overview

Elara is a mobile web app for aesthetic clinics. It shows patients a realistic AI preview of their treatment on their own face and keeps every photo as an organized, GDPR-conscious record. I started with field research at Sunsolfigueres - Centre d'estètica, observing their processes on site. Two things stood out:

Patients ask for the final result at the first visit. Privacy rules out other patients' photos.

The doctor keeps every photo on her phone, hard to search.

Elara answers both: a preview on the patient's own face, and photos organized per patient and treatment.

Problem definition

In a consultation, results are explained with words. Patients can't picture it on their own face, and the usual examples don't look like them or can't be shown. So expectations get unrealistic and decisions slow down.

1

Patients can't picture the result

Words, sketches and generic galleries don't show their own face.

2

Doctors can't show real examples

Photos of other patients can't be shared for privacy reasons.

3

Clinical photos are hard to manage

They sit in a phone gallery, inconsistent and hard to search.

Goals

Primary goals
  • Let patients see a credible preview of their treatment, on their own face, during the consultation.
  • Give the doctor one place where every patient's photos are organized and easy to find.
Secondary goals
  • Capture consistent clinical photos: same pose, light and expression.
  • Protect patient data from day one: consent, EU hosting, automatic deletion.
  • Support the conversation without overpromising: results are labeled as estimates.

The consultation flow

A guided flow of six steps takes the doctor from patient data to an estimated result, on a phone, with the patient present.

1

Patient data

Name, surname and clinic code.

One record per patient
2

Treatment area

Tap the zone on an anatomical face map.

Precise, no free text
3

Treatment

Choose the treatment per zone, and the brand for neuromodulators.

Brands give different results
4

Consent

Three clauses and a signature.

No photo before consent
5

Guided photo

Live camera with a face guide and checks for level and light.

Better photo, better simulation
6

Estimated result

A draggable before and after slider, labeled as an AI estimate.

Sets expectations

AFTER THE CONSULTATION

Notes and consent

Product, dose and lot per treatment, plus the signed consent as a PDF.

Statistics

Patients and treatments over time, a face map of treated areas and top treatments.

Key design decisions

1

Guided capture

Live camera with a face guide per photo type. Checks phone level, light and light symmetry. Follow-ups overlay the original photo as a ghost.

3:4 frame · live checks
2

Consent gate

No photo before a signed consent. The consent text is versioned and the signature is valid for 2 years, with an explanation when it asks again.

Versioned · 2 years
3

Anatomical face map

Zones are selected on a face map instead of free text. Multi-select, mapped to the treatments available per zone.

11 zones · 30 treatments
4

Treatment to photo mapping

Each treatment requires a specific photo type and the server rejects mismatches. Found when a raised-eyebrow photo with a frown prompt changed the expression.

10 photo types

Privacy and security

  • Private photos, hosted in the EU
  • Consent before any photo
  • Logs without photos, names or notes
  • Photos deleted after 2 years or on request

Outcomes and learnings

View live app
  • Live: an installable app running on EU infrastructure.
  • Pilot ready: separate clinic accounts, access log, simulation limits and deletion rules.
  • In closed beta: testing the flow and the simulation quality with test users.

Learning

This was my first project built entirely with Claude, and the first time I took a product all the way to production. Decisions I would normally hand to engineers, like what happens offline or what gets deleted and when, became mine to make. I learned that designing with AI means designing its limits as carefully as the interface.
